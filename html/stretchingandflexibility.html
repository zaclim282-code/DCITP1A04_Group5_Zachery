Why Do We Stretch?

Stretching and mobility work are essential for maintaining healthy, functional joints and muscles. When we exercise, our muscles contract and undergo stress. These issues may seem unimportant, but without a proper stretching routine:

Injury risk increases

Stiff muscles and limited joint range are far more susceptible to injuries such as strain or tearing.

Performance drops

Limited mobility in joints and muscles may decrease efficiency when doing exercises.

Types of Stretching

Not all stretching is always suitable. Using the wrong type of stretch at the wrong time can decrease performance or increase injury risk.

1. Warm-up: Dynamic Workout (pre-workout)

Effect: Prepare the central nervous system, increase heart rate, send blood flow to target muscles, and warm up joint synovial fluid.

Why it prevents injury: Cold muscles are brittle. Dynamic movements prime muscles through functional ranges of motion without weakening muscular power output before intense workouts.

Duration: Moving continuously (10–15 reps per movement)

2. Cool-Down: Static Stretching (Post-Workout)

Effect: Bring heart rate back down, elongate warm muscle fibres, promotes recovery.

Why it prevents injury: After workouts, muscle fibres are shortened and inflamed. Holding static stretches signals the nervous system to relax and helps reset baseline muscle length, reducing chronic stiffness over time.

Duration: Holding positions (15-30 seconds per muscle)
